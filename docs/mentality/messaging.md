# Messaging

How Colonaiz talks about itself. Decisions the founder made on 14–15 November 2026.

## Tone

Headlines should sound like business, technology, AI and space technology.

We are professional business analysts, and the page text should read like a business analyst talking to the visitor.

## Why space

The founder's explanation, 18 November 2026. The site shows spacecraft, stars and planets for a reason, and the copy should tell the visitor that reason.

- The client's business is the body at the centre.
- The data about that business (market, competitors, industry) must be gathered into a ring around it, an orbit of 1s and 0s.
- With that ring in place the client is fully aware and builds the product with awareness.

Copy should use space elements (orbit, navigation, spacecraft) with a touch of sarcasm, and map back to this idea rather than use space as decoration. The "What we do" headline carries it.

## Wording rules

- Do not say "AI agents" do the work. It sounds like trash. Say our business analysts do it, using cutting-edge technology. Exception from the founder on 22 November 2026: the last step of "How it works" ("An analyst reviews it", step 5 since 23 November 2026) does name the agentic system and the RAG, because there the point is that a specialist always checks the final result, so no AI slop reaches the client. Whether the founder still wants the rule elsewhere on the page is not confirmed.
- Do not ask the client to add their competitors. We find them.
- Do not state the page count of the report. Use words that convey a full, substantial document.
- Do not say "study". Use a word that conveys investigation and accuracy, such as "investigate".
- Do not commit to "every week". Use wording that suggests a regular rhythm without a seven-day promise. Since 22 November 2026 the rhythm is known: the founder calls the regular reports "monthly updates", so "every month" may be said where the 12-month plan is described.
- Frame the outcome as competitors never getting one step ahead of the client, rather than the client being one step ahead.
- Keep sentences short: use precise words that deliver the exact meaning with higher impact.
- Copy that describes the visitor (such as the "Who are our clients?" point, formerly "If this is you") must sound professional and create a need in them. Write something every founder feels.

## What the client wants to know

The page must answer these:

1. What will they get? A PDF full of reports, charts, facts and data.
2. Why is it worth it?
3. When will they receive the document?
4. How complete is the document?
5. Are we a legitimate business?

## FAQ

Set by the founder on 22 November 2026.

- The FAQ has two jobs. First, answer the key questions that would stop a client from buying. Second, carry questions that help SEO.
- The answers must be highly valuable, technical information, so that Google and LLMs see we are highly skilled in this field. The founder gave his own answers as considerations and asked for the best, technical version of each.
- Voice for this content: it must read as written by a person, not generated. The founder's description: like someone with IELTS 7.5 wrote it, with slightly imperfect grammar. (How the agent applied it: plain words, direct opinions, a few loose constructions; no deliberate spelling mistakes.)
- The founder's points, as he gave them:
  - Finding competitors: most people search with ChatGPT, Claude and similar; their data may be outdated, and a small business needs the others at its exact scale that are working silently. Some solutions keep a database and search through vectors to find similar websites. We monitor the listing websites (Product Hunt, G2, AlternativeTo and other famous ones) to build a big dataset of domains: more than 300,000 unique domains, one per startup, the biggest startup database compared with every one of them. Beside it we have an active crawling system that indexes new startups, using our existing databases and the explorer system we developed.
  - Tracking pricing: the plans and subscriptions of each website must be checked actively. Doing it manually is frustrating and time-consuming; our tools give a polished, fresh analysis from many similar websites and a better vision from the aggregated pricing data.
  - How often: because of the AI boom, tons of startups are created every day and the market reshapes every month. Teams that plan in sprints should get a market analysis every month to plan the next sprint better.
  - What a report should include: hiring, product changes, new features, prices and plans, team size, the market they work in, social media engagement, the country, the number of sales they report, blog post updates, website traffic, backlinks, and other factors that can measure a business.
  - Cost: expensive solutions are not affordable for small and medium companies, especially early, and may contain noise or outdated information; cheap ones do not cover all the factors. Star Track was built to give small and medium companies the best information at an affordable price.
- Not answered yet by the founder, so not on the page: what happens if we pick the wrong competitors, and how soon the first report arrives.
- The lock-in and confidentiality questions were added on 22 November 2026, written by the agent from the rules the founder confirmed for the terms (no automatic renewal, refund in the first month only, refund of undelivered reports if we stop Star Track). The confidentiality answer rests on clauses the agent added to the terms; not yet confirmed by the founder.
- The Delivery section now says a new report arrives every month.
