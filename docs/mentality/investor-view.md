# Investor view

An outside evaluation of Colonaiz as a VC would see it, shared by the founder on 23 November 2026. It is not the founder's own wording. Which parts the founder adopts is noted under each area; where nothing is noted, treat it as criticism our product and website have to answer, not as decided strategy.

The evaluation names the three weakest areas of the profile. Each one is a different question.

## 1. Product differentiation: 4/10

The question: why would customers choose Colonaiz?

- Colonaiz appears to combine existing technologies: web crawling and data extraction, AI summarization, RAG and semantic search, startup discovery, automated market research reports, competitor monitoring. None of them is unique by itself. The opportunity is in how they are combined.
- Recommended positioning: continuous discovery of business opportunities, rather than market research.
- The example given: a user asks for emerging AI companies developing supply-chain optimization in Europe that have not yet received significant investment. Colonaiz should discover relevant companies, understand their products, identify relationships, assess market gaps, and explain why they represent opportunities. That is worth more than a list of websites.
- The recommendation: build the product around proprietary discovery and intelligence, not AI-generated reports.

Adopted so far: the founder took the middle of that example into the process on 23 November 2026 as "company evaluation and processing" (see [product.md](product.md)). The positioning is now part of [product.md](product.md) too ("How we position it"), added the same day when the founder asked for the conflict between this evaluation and our docs to be fixed.

## 2. Defensibility: 4/10

The question: why couldn't competitors easily copy it? The evaluation calls this probably the most important concern for an investor.

- Easy for a competitor to reproduce: a web crawler, an LLM summarization pipeline, vector embeddings, a RAG chatbot, automated PDF generation. These are implementation choices, not advantages.
- Four possible moats, with their strategic value: a proprietary historical dataset (high), an entity relationship graph (very high), continuous change detection (high), customer feedback and intelligence enrichment (high).
- The moat the evaluation would pursue: a continuously updated business intelligence graph. Instead of storing isolated summaries, extract and connect companies and subsidiaries, founders and executives, products and technologies, investors and funding rounds, markets and locations, competitors and partnerships, and historical changes.
- Why it defends: a competitor can copy the software architecture, but years of normalized historical data, entity resolution and relationship history are much harder to recreate.
- The caveat: a large dataset alone is not a moat. The data must be accurate, differentiated, continuously maintained, and useful to customers.

The size of the data, wherever this evaluation is used or quoted, is our own figure: a database of 300,000+ unique startup domains (see [product.md](product.md)). The evaluation's example used other numbers (200,000 pages across 140,000 unique domains); the founder asked for the mismatch to be fixed on 23 November 2026, and those numbers are not ours and are not to be repeated.

## 3. Commercial validation: 3/10

The question: is there evidence that customers want it and pay for it?

- Pricing and product descriptions on a website do not demonstrate product-market fit.
- The evidence an investor wants: paying customers (critical), repeat purchases (critical), monthly recurring revenue, customer retention, customer acquisition cost and usage frequency (all high), testimonials and case studies (medium).
- The $99 report and the $999 annual plan are reasonable hypotheses, but it is not known whether customers will pay these prices.
- Targets before seeking investment or applying for a startup visa:
  - 5 to 10 paying B2B customers.
  - 3 documented customer case studies.
  - Evidence of repeat usage.
  - At least one customer using the intelligence for an actual investment, partnership, or strategic decision.
- Even a small number of genuine paying customers strengthens the business case significantly.
